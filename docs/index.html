Contoso Retail: Inventory, Discount and Margin Analysis

3.4M sales lines and 8.0M weekly inventory records across 306 stores and 2,500 products, 2007 to 2009. Analysis in SQL (DuckDB) and Python.

About the data. Contoso is Microsoft's fictitious retail sample data warehouse. The fields are real (cost, discount, returns, on-hand, on-order, safety stock), but the records are synthetic, so the figures show method, not a real business. Amounts are in the dataset's own currency units. Inventory conclusions use the latest weekly snapshot (26 Dec 2009).

Key findings

  • Discounting costs margin and does not always buy volume. Full-price lines earn 57.6% gross margin. The Spring 20% promotion earns 55.5% and sells 14.6 units per line against 14.8 at full price, so it gives up margin without a volume gain. The Holiday 15% promotion lifts units per line to 20.9 at 56.0% margin.
  • Over a third of inventory value is slow. 28.7M of 78.5M sits in store-products with more than 26 weeks of cover or no sales in the last 13 weeks. Home Appliances and Computers hold the largest amounts.
  • Shortages are concentrated. Music, Movies and Audio Books has 67% of store-products below safety stock with no order placed. Cell phone accessories are the biggest reorder exposure by weekly demand.
  • Sales fell each year while margin held near 57%. Net sales dropped from 4.56B in 2007 to 3.74B in 2009, yet units sold rose from 17.0M to 19.8M. Revenue per unit fell about 30%, which points to cheaper products or lower prices rather than weaker demand. The data does not separate price from mix.
